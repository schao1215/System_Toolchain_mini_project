The four roles

The roles follow how the work is graded: the evidence script's Detectors block is roles 1 and 2, and its Evaluation and diagnosis block is roles 3 and 4. They also follow the code's own file boundaries.

01

PCA monitor

T² and SPE
Code you own
data.py (checksums, standardization with ddof=1 on runs 1–300), pca_monitor.py, plots.py
Lectures
  • L8: "Ridge, and why scaling matters now". The scaler is fitted on training rows only.
  • L11: "Adam on raw inputs". The same rule, measured.
  • PCA itself isn't taught in any lecture. The brief's primer is the core material.
Further reading
  • Russell, Chiang & Braatz (2000): the T² and Q (SPE) definitions and control limits
  • De Ketelaere, Hubert & Schmitt: the clearest open derivation of both limits
  • BibMon's TEP tutorial: a worked example on this plant
  • Any short PCA explainer, if eigenvectors are rusty
All but the last are linked under Resources on the brief page.
Notes
  • The code uses sklearn PCA(svd_solver="full"). The brief says this gives the same model as eigh of the covariance. Eigenvector signs can flip; T² and SPE don't change.
  • k is the first position where the cumulative variance ratio reaches 0.90 (searchsorted).
  • T² divides by each eigenvalue, so a move along a normally quiet direction counts for more.
  • T² is weak on faults 4, 5, 10 and 11. The controllers pull the operating point back inside the normal cloud.

02

Forecast-residual monitor

Ridge on lags t−1, t−2
Code you own
ridge_monitor.py (lag_design, fit_ridge, score_ridge)
Lectures
  • L7: lag features, ARX, and the run-boundary / group-key rule
  • L8: "Time series as supervised learning", "Model families and multi-step strategies", "Residuals: diagnosis and detection"
  • L9: "Regularization: ridge and lasso", for what alpha does
Further reading
Brief: "Forecast monitor"; L8 is the source the brief points to
Notes
  • The model is L8's one-channel detector for all 52 channels at once: 104 features, 52 targets, one Ridge(alpha=1.0).
  • Run numbers 1–20 repeat for every fault, so a lag must stay inside the same (faultNumber, simulationRun) pair. lag_design also checks that sample numbers are consecutive.
  • Each residual is divided by that channel's training residual std, so a channel that is noisy in normal operation doesn't dominate.
  • Samples 1–2 of every run have no score, giving 298,800 scored rows.
  • L8 warned that a one-step residual usually shrinks once the plant settles after a step. Fault 5 doesn't do that here: ridge stays at 0.996. That's the interesting thing to explain.

03

Evaluation and submission

thresholds, alarms, detection table
Code you own
evaluate.py (validation_threshold, alarm_mask, detection_rows), pipeline.py (the self-checks _check_alarm_rule, _assert_score_rows)
Lectures
  • L8: "Residuals: diagnosis and detection". A 99th-percentile threshold gave 4.8 false alarms a day; 3 in a row brought that to 0.01 a day, for at least 6 extra minutes of delay.
  • L8: "Evaluating on time", the part on splitting by series
  • L9: "Training, validation and test sets", "Choosing, then testing once"
  • L10: "Measuring a classifier" (confusion matrix, precision/recall, moving the threshold)
Further reading
Brief: "Evaluation (second week)", "Submit", "Grading" (the list of scripted checks)
Notes
  • Detection rate is recall per fault. The fault-0 row is the false-alarm side.
  • Delay = (first alarm sample − 20) × 3 minutes. Take the median over runs that alarmed, and count runs missed.
  • T² on faults 3, 9 and 15 alarms once (at 231 min) in 1 of 20 runs. Whether that counts as "not detected" is decided by the script, so run it.
  • CAUGHT_RATE = 0.10 in config.py is our own reporting cut-off, not from the brief. Say so in the report.
